Desk brief

Inflation is cooling unevenly across the rich world

Price growth has settled at target in the euro area. America and Britain are proving stickier, and the desk's alert bands now reflect that.

Rates desk, October 30th 2026. Six-minute read.

Two years after central banks declared the worst of the post-pandemic price surge over, inflation in the rich world has settled into an awkward pattern. In the euro area it has hovered around the 2% target since the spring of 2025. In America and Britain it has refused to get there, drifting between 2.5% and 3% as services prices, rents and wages have cooled only slowly.

The divergence matters for the desk because it changes what a surprise means. A 0.1-point miss in Frankfurt is noise; the same miss in Washington feeds straight into the pricing of the next two Federal Reserve meetings. The watchlist bands have been recalibrated accordingly: the euro-area series now trips an alert only outside 1.7–2.3%, while the American and British series use a tighter 2.4–2.9% band around the desk's own forecast.

Growth tells a similar story (see chart). America is still expected to expand by close to 2% this year, more than the euro area and Britain combined on a per-head basis, and with less slack in its labour market. That is the main reason the desk expects American core inflation to be the last of the three to return to target.

What to watch next week: the October payrolls report on Friday, the Bank of England's decision on Thursday (a hold is priced), and flash euro-area inflation on Wednesday. This brief will be updated after each release.
